Where we actually are

You're probably picturing a chatbot.
We're moving past that now.

Most people still think AI means asking a chatbot a question and getting an answer back. It can do a lot more than that now. The latest tools don't just reply with text: they can use your other software, open and work with your files, and build things for you. That's the shift worth paying attention to.

03 / 09
The way to think about AI

Think of it as
your own assistant.

Imagine you have your own assistant: someone you delegate tasks to. Anything you'd hand to an assistant, try it on the AI. And like anyone new, you wouldn't expect it to nail it first time without feedback. You show it how you work, and the training honestly isn't much effort.

A few things worth knowing:

Never forgets

Brain-dump everything you know onto it and it never forgets.

Endless patience

Ask it to redo something five times, or change direction halfway through. It never gets tired or takes it personally, it just tries again.

You stay in charge

You decide what it does and what it doesn't. The final sign-off on anything client-facing stays yours.

Let's actually try one

Let it interview you.

The biggest difference I've found isn't a clever prompt. It's giving the AI enough context about you and your role. So instead of explaining all that, let it ask. Paste this in and answer honestly.

"Before we do any work together, I want you to interview me so you understand my role properly. Ask me one question at a time. Cover what I do day to day, who my clients and audience are, what I'm responsible for, what 'good' looks like in my work, the parts I find tedious, and how I like things done. Keep going until you genuinely understand my role, then summarise back everything you've learned so I can save it and reuse it. Don't move on from a question until the answer is clear."

Then keep the summary. Paste it into your AI's memory or custom instructions, or a doc you reuse, so it never starts from zero again.

A few good habits

Nothing scary. Just a bit of good hygiene.

These habits will let us use AI on real work without anyone getting nervous.

Data security

Where you can, take client details or anything sensitive out before they go into AI. And the safety net: on a proper enterprise account, nothing you put in is used to train future models, so it shouldn't leak out.

Transparency

Be open about when AI helped draft something, rather than passing it off as something it wasn't.

Human in the loop

A human checks anything client-facing. Verify the facts and figures, AI can sound confident even when it's wrong.

Keep it in the open

Flag new tools in one place so IT can check them and we all learn from each other.

08 / 09
An open invite

It gets better if we share it.

If you find a tool or workflow that helps you, it almost certainly helps someone else too. The whole thing gets better the more we pool it.

An opt-in channel

I'd like to start a Slack channel for this. Totally optional. I'd post what I build and find; you post whatever you like.

Wins and flops

Share what worked, and what didn't. Someone might have a fix, or we just learn where AI falls down together.

Honest and transparent

That's the whole game. As long as we're open about it, we figure out the right way to use this as a team.

Try one thing: pick something you do on repeat and don't enjoy, then see how much AI can help you with it.
