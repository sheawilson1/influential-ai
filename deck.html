02 / 08
Using it well

Use it freely.
Just keep it in the open.

01

Be transparent

Say when AI helped, with the team and with clients where it matters. Passing AI work off as something it wasn't is how trust breaks. "AI drafted this, I shaped it" is how it builds.

02

Keep it in one conversation

Trying a new tool, or putting client data near one? Flag it first, in one channel, so IT can vet it and everyone learns from each other. Not fifteen people on fifteen private logins.

03

A human reviews everything

AI drafts, a person decides. Every client-facing output passes human eyes before it leaves the building.

The deal

Use AI as much as you like. Keep it open, keep it vetted, keep a human in the loop. That's how we get the speed without the risk.

03 / 08
The rule that doesn't change

AI is the accelerator.
You are still the brain.

Everything that ships to a client passes a human first. Not because AI gets things wrong (it often doesn't), but because judgement, taste, and accountability aren't outsourceable.

Verify

Facts, figures, claims, attributions. AI is confident even when it's wrong.

Edit

Tone, nuance, the specific way Influential sounds. AI gets close, not there.

Approve

Sign-off is human. Always. No matter how good the draft.

From my placement so far

Things I didn't know
AI could actually do.

Design ingestion

Brand guidelines to Figma, in minutes

Fed the full brand doc into AI. It built a structured, organised Figma page mapping every colour, font, and rule. Saved hours of manual setup.

Custom tooling

I built a Figma plugin without writing code

Had a tedious border-inconsistency problem. AI wrote the plugin to fix it. A bespoke tool, built for one specific annoying job.

Editor mode

AI as a brutal sounding board

Not for generating. For reviewing. Spot the logical gap, the weak argument, the thing I missed. Better than a polite reviewer.

06 / 08
Now we play

Pick a game.
No marketing. No pressure.

The point isn't to make you do more work in a workshop. It's to get you comfortable poking at AI until it does something surprising. Pick one.

A

Prompt Battle

Start with a bland, useless AI output. Teams refine the prompt to coax the most impressive response. Best result wins.

B

Inventing the Absurd

Conceptualise a completely useless product. Generate specs, a PR crisis, a launch pitch. Inclusive of every department.

C

Build a Planet

Invent a planet from scratch. Ecosystem, inhabitants, laws, language. Tests how well AI holds complex context.

Where this leaves us

The team that experiments
wins the next 18 months.

You don't need to become an engineer. You need to stop being afraid of the blank prompt box.

Try one thing this week

Pick a task you hate. Try to get AI to take 30% of it off you.

Share what worked

The best prompts and tricks should circulate. Slack channel for it.

Push enterprise access

Once we have safe tools, we can stop being cautious about real client work.
